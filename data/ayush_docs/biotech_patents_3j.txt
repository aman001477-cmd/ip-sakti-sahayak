BIOTECHNOLOGY PATENTS IN INDIA — SECTION 3(j) AND MICRO-ORGANISMS
Patents Act, Section 3(j) | Budapest Treaty | PPV&FR Interface

1. THE LAW
Section 3(j) excludes "plants and animals in whole or any part thereof OTHER THAN
MICRO-ORGANISMS but including seeds, varieties and species and essentially biological processes
for production or propagation of plants and animals". Consequences:
(a) MICRO-ORGANISMS (bacteria, fungi, cell lines, plasmids, novel strains) CAN be patented if
    new, inventive and industrially applicable.
(b) PLANTS, ANIMALS, SEEDS, VARIETIES cannot be patented — plant varieties go to the sui generis
    PPV&FR Act, 2001 instead (TRIPS Article 27.3(b) option).
(c) ESSENTIALLY BIOLOGICAL PROCESSES (conventional crossing/breeding) are excluded; MICROBIOLOGICAL
    and non-biological processes (e.g., genetic transformation methods) are patentable subject
    matter.

2. DIMMINACO-TYPE PRINCIPLE
Biotech processes producing live end-products (e.g., processes for preparing vaccines involving
live micro-organisms) have been held patentable where they involve human intervention beyond
essentially biological steps — the process, and novel micro-organisms themselves, are not barred
by Section 3(j).

3. BT COTTON LITIGATION (MONSANTO v. NUZIVEEDU — NOTE THE NUANCE)
Monsanto's Bt gene-construct patents and the Section 3(j) exclusion were litigated up to the
Supreme Court (2019), which set aside a summary invalidation and remanded full trial — leaving
the patent-vs-3(j) and patent-vs-PPVFR boundary to evidence-led adjudication. The episode shows
how Section 3(j), the PPV&FR Act and the Seeds/Price-Control orders intersect in agri-biotech.

4. BUDAPEST TREATY (DEPOSIT OF MICRO-ORGANISMS)
India is party to the Budapest Treaty (1977/1980): where an invention involves a micro-organism
not publicly available and undescribable in writing, sufficiency of disclosure (Section 10) is met
by depositing it with an International Depository Authority (India's IMTECH Chandigarh is one).
Reference the accession number in the specification.

5. AYUSH/BIO INTERFACE
Fermentation-derived Ayurvedic processes using characterised novel microbial strains, engineered
enzymes for herbal extraction, and microbiological assays sit on the patentable side; mere use of
naturally occurring microbes or classical formulations does not.

6. KEYWORDS
Section 3(j), micro-organisms patentable, essentially biological vs microbiological processes,
Budapest Treaty deposit, IMTECH, Dimminaco principle, Bt cotton, PPVFR interface, seeds not
patentable, cell lines.

JEEVANI / AROGYAPACHA — INDIA'S FIRST ACCESS AND BENEFIT-SHARING SUCCESS
Kani Tribe, TBGRI and CSIR | Traditional Knowledge and ABS

1. THE KNOWLEDGE
The Kani tribal community of the Agasthyamalai hills (Kerala/Tamil Nadu) traditionally used the
plant Arogyapacha (Trichopus zeylanicus) as an energy-giving, anti-fatigue remedy. Tribal guides
shared this knowledge with scientists of the Tropical Botanic Garden and Research Institute
(TBGRI, Trivandrum) during ethnobotanical surveys in the late 1980s.

2. THE SCIENCE
TBGRI and later CSIR laboratories (including CDRI Lucknow and RRL Jammu) validated the plant's
adaptogenic, anti-stress and immuno-enhancing properties and developed "Jeevani", a polyherbal
formulation, with Arya Vaidya Pharmacy (Coimbatore) licensed for manufacture.

3. THE BENEFIT-SHARING MODEL
A pioneering arrangement: licence fees and royalties were shared with the Kani community through
a dedicated community trust fund (about half of the licence/royalty stream earmarked for tribal
welfare, development and biodiversity conservation). This became the world's most-cited early
example of ABS working in practice — years before the CBD's Bonn Guidelines (2002) and the
Nagoya Protocol (2010).

4. WHY IT MATTERS FOR IP-SAKTI
(a) Shows the POSITIVE side of TK commercialisation: documented consent + scientific value
    addition + shared revenue, instead of biopiracy.
(b) Blueprint for Biodiversity Act compliance: prior approval, mutually agreed terms, community
    funds, BMC involvement under today's NBA/SBB framework.
(c) Relevant to AYUSH industry partnerships with tribal and rural knowledge holders.

5. CONTRAST WITH BIOPIRACY
Unlike the Neem/Turmeric patent challenges (defensive victories AFTER misappropriation was
attempted), Jeevani was collaborative FROM THE START — the model the Nagoya Protocol now
mandates globally.

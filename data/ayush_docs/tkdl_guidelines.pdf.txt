TRADITIONAL KNOWLEDGE DIGITAL LIBRARY (TKDL) - COMPREHENSIVE GUIDE
Ministry of Ayush, Government of India

CHAPTER I: INTRODUCTION

1.1 What is TKDL?
The Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL) is a pioneering initiative of the Government of India to prevent misappropriation of India's traditional knowledge and to protect India's traditional medicines from biopiracy. It is a joint initiative of the Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) and the Department of Ayurveda, Yoga & Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and Homoeopathy (AYUSH).

1.2 Objectives of TKDL
The main objectives of TKDL are:
(a) To prevent grant of patents on non-original inventions based on Indian traditional knowledge
(b) To digitize and document traditional knowledge in a structured format
(c) To make traditional knowledge accessible to patent examiners worldwide
(d) To protect India's traditional heritage from misappropriation
(e) To promote research and development based on traditional knowledge

1.3 Need for TKDL
India has a rich heritage of traditional knowledge spanning thousands of years. This knowledge has been passed down through generations orally and in ancient texts. However, this knowledge was not documented in a format accessible to patent examiners. As a result, many patent applications were filed based on Indian traditional knowledge without proper credit or benefit sharing.

1.4 Success of TKDL
Since its establishment, TKDL has been instrumental in preventing biopiracy:
- Over 200 patent applications have been rejected or withdrawn
- Major cases include Neem, Turmeric, Basmati, Karela, Jamun, Brinjal
- TKDL has been recognized as a model for traditional knowledge protection worldwide

CHAPTER II: TKDL DATABASE

2.1 Content of TKDL
The TKDL database contains information from traditional Indian medical systems:

Ayurveda:
- ~100,000 formulations from classical texts
- Charaka Samhita
- Sushruta Samhita
- Ashtanga Hridayam
- Madhava Nidanam
- Sharangadhara Samhita
- Bhavaprakasha Nighantu
- Dhanvantari Nighantu

Unani:
- ~20,000 formulations
- Al-Qanoon Fil Tibb (Canon of Medicine)
- Akseer-e-Azam
- Makhzanul Mufradat
- Kitab-ul-Hawi

Siddha:
- ~10,000 formulations
- Siddha Vaidya texts
- Traditional Tamil medical literature

Homoeopathy:
- Repertories and materia medica
- Classical homoeopathic remedies

2.2 Structure of TKDL
Each entry in TKDL contains:
(a) Classical reference
(b) Name of the formulation
(c) Ingredients
(d) Process of preparation
(e) Indications
(f) Dose
(g) Mode of administration
(h) Contraindications
(i) Reference text

2.3 Access to TKDL

2.4 Format of TKDL

CHAPTER III: FAMOUS CASES

3.1 Turmeric Patent Case
Background:
In 1995, the University of Mississippi Medical Center obtained a US patent (US 5,401,504) for the use of turmeric in wound healing.

India's Challenge:
The Indian Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) challenged the patent on the grounds that:
- Turmeric has been used for wound healing in India for centuries
- The use is documented in ancient texts like Charaka Samhita
- The patent was for a known use, not a new invention

Outcome:
The US Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) revoked the patent in 1997, recognizing that the use of turmeric for wound healing was prior art.

3.2 Neem Patent Case
Background:
In 1995, the US Department of Agriculture and W.R. Grace Company obtained a European Patent (EP 0436257) for a method of controlling fungi on plants using neem oil.

India's Challenge:
India challenged the patent on the grounds that:
- Neem has been used as a fungicide in India for centuries
- The use is documented in ancient texts
- The patent was for a known use, not a new invention

Outcome:
The European Patent Office (EPO) revoked the patent in 2005, recognizing that the use of neem oil as a fungicide was prior art.

3.3 Basmati Rice Patent Case
Background:
In 1994, RiceTec Inc. obtained a US patent (US 5,663,484) for a type of rice similar to Basmati.

India's Challenge:
India challenged the patent on the grounds that:
- Basmati is a traditional variety grown in India for centuries
- The patent was for a known variety, not a new invention
- The patent infringed India's geographical indications

Outcome:
The USPTO invalidated most of the claims in 2001, recognizing that Basmati is a traditional variety.

3.4 Karela (Bitter Gourd) Patent Case
Background:
A patent was filed for the use of Karela in treating diabetes.

India's Challenge:
India challenged the patent on the grounds that:
- Karela has been used for treating diabetes in India for centuries
- The use is documented in ancient texts like Charaka Samhita
- The patent was for a known use, not a new invention

Outcome:
The patent was rejected based on TKDL evidence.

3.5 Jamun Patent Case
Background:

India's Challenge:
India challenged the patent on the grounds that:
- Jamun has been used for treating diabetes in India for centuries
- The use is documented in ancient texts

Outcome:
The patent was rejected based on TKDL evidence.

CHAPTER IV: PATENT EXAMINER ACCESS

4.1 How Patent Examinators Use TKDL
Patent examiners worldwide use TKDL to:
(a) Search for prior art
(b) Evaluate novelty of inventions
(c) Assess inventive step
(d) Determine patentability

4.2 Search Strategy
Patent examiners typically search TKDL using:
(a) Keywords related to the invention
(b) Classification codes
(c) Traditional names of formulations
(d) Ingredients and their properties

4.3 Evidence from TKDL
TKDL provides evidence in the form of:
(a) Classical text references
(b) Traditional usage documentation
(e) Formulation details
(f) Historical evidence

4.4 Impact on Patent Examination
TKDL has significantly impacted patent examination:
(a) More accurate prior art searches
(b) Better evaluation of novelty
(c) Proper assessment of inventive step
(d) Reduced grant of patents based on traditional knowledge

CHAPTER V: GUIDELINES FOR PATENT APPLICANTS

5.1 Duty of Disclosure
Patent applicants have a duty to disclose:
(a) All known prior art
(b) All relevant traditional knowledge
(c) All related publications
(d) All related patent applications

5.2 Consequences of Non-Disclosure
Failure to disclose relevant information may result in:
(a) Rejection of patent application
(b) Revocation of granted patent
(c) Legal proceedings
(d) Penalties

5.3 How to Search TKDL
Applicants can search TKDL through:
(a) The TKDL website
(b) Patent office databases
(c) Licensed access providers
(d) Government agencies

5.4 Best Practices
Applicants should:
(a) Conduct thorough prior art searches
(b) Document all traditional knowledge references
(c) Properly cite all sources
(d) Comply with all disclosure requirements

CHAPTER VI: BENEFIT SHARING

6.1 What is Benefit Sharing?
Benefit sharing refers to the fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from the use of traditional knowledge.

6.2 Legal Framework
Benefit sharing is mandated by:
(a) The Biodiversity Act, 2002
(b) The Patent Act, 1970
(c) International agreements
(d) National policies

6.3 Types of Benefits
Benefits may include:
(a) Financial benefits
(b) Non-financial benefits
(c) Technology transfer
(d) Capacity building
(e) Research collaboration

6.4 Mechanism for Benefit Sharing
Benefit sharing may be achieved through:
(a) Access agreements
(b) Licensing agreements
(c) Joint ventures
(d) Research partnerships
(e) Community development programs

APPENDIX B: HOW TO CITE TKDL

Format:

Traditional System: [Ayurveda/Unani/Siddha/Homoeopathy]
Classical Text: [Name of Text]
Chapter: [Chapter Number]
Sutra/Shloka: [Sutra/Shloka Number]
Formulation Name: [Name]

Indications: [List of Indications]

Example:
TKDL Entry No: 12345
Traditional System: Ayurveda
Classical Text: Charaka Samhita
Chapter: Chikitsasthana
Sutra/Shloka: 1/2/3
Formulation Name: Triphala Churna
Ingredients: Amalaki, Bibhitaki, Haritaki
Indications: Digestive disorders, Eye diseases

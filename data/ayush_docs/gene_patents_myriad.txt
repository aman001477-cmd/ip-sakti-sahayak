GENE PATENTING WORLDWIDE — MYRIAD (USA) AND D'ARCY (AUSTRALIA)
Comparative Biotech Patent Law | Relevance for India's Section 3(c)/3(j)

1. ASSOCIATION FOR MOLECULAR PATHOLOGY v. MYRIAD GENETICS (US SUPREME COURT, 2013)
Myriad held patents on isolated BRCA1/BRCA2 gene sequences for breast-cancer testing. The Court
held UNANIMOUSLY:
(a) Merely ISOLATED genomic DNA is a product of nature and NOT patent-eligible — separation
    from the genome does not create an invention.
(b) Synthetically created COMPLIMENTARY DNA (cDNA) IS patent-eligible because a lab technician
    creates something new.
The decision ended gene-sequence monopolies on diagnostics while preserving engineered-construct
patents.

2. D'ARCY v. MYRIAD GENETICS (HIGH COURT OF AUSTRALIA, 2015)
On the same BRCA patents, Australia's apex court held unanimously that isolated nucleic acids —
even separated from cells — are NOT a "manner of manufacture": the substance and information are
the same as in nature, so no patentable invention exists.

3. EUROPEAN POSITION
Under the EU Biotech Directive (98/44/EC), an isolated element of the human body INCLUDING gene
sequences CAN be patented where a specific industrial application is disclosed — a middle path
between the US/Australian rejections and permissive practice.

4. INDIAN LENS (COMMENTARY POSITION)
(a) Section 3(c) bars mere discovery of living/non-living substances occurring in nature —
    commentators generally view bare isolated human gene sequences as falling here.
(b) Section 3(j) bars plants/animals/seeds but EXCEPTS micro-organisms; engineered constructs,
    vectors, modified cell lines and microbiological processes remain patentable.
(c) Diagnostic METHOD claims face Section 3(i); diagnostic KITS as products are examined on
    novelty, inventive step and industrial application.

5. KEYWORDS
Myriad BRCA, isolated DNA product of nature, cDNA patentable, D'Arcy Australia manner of
manufacture, EU Biotech Directive, Section 3(c), gene sequence, diagnostics.

PARIS CONVENTION (1883) — PRIORITY AND NATIONAL TREATMENT EXPLAINED
Paris Convention for the Protection of Industrial Property | International IP Framework

1. WHAT IT IS
The Paris Convention (1883, administered by WIPO) is the foundational treaty for patents,
trademarks and industrial designs, with 180+ member countries including India. Much of the
Indian Patents Act's international machinery (Sections 133–135 on convention countries and
priority) implements this treaty.

2. NATIONAL TREATMENT (ARTICLE 2)
Nationals of any member country enjoy the same IP protection in every other member country as
that country's own nationals — no discrimination against foreigners.

3. RIGHT OF PRIORITY (ARTICLE 4) — THE CORE FOR PATENT FILERS
(a) A patent application first filed in one member country gives a 12-MONTH priority window
    (6 months for designs and trademarks) to file in other member countries.
(b) The later filings are treated AS IF filed on the first (priority) date — intervening
    publications or filings by others cannot destroy novelty.
(c) Indian practice: claim convention priority via Form 1 + priority documents; India also
    recognises PCT international filing dates for national-phase entries.

4. INDEPENDENCE OF PATENTS (ARTICLE 4bis)
A patent granted in one country is independent of patents for the same invention elsewhere —
rejection or revocation in one country does not affect others.

5. COMPULSORY LICENCES (ARTICLE 5A)
The Convention expressly permits compulsory licences to prevent patent abuses (e.g., failure to
work) — the international ancestor of India's Section 84/92 regime — with safeguards such as
waiting periods (4 years from filing or 3 years from grant).

6. OTHER PILLARS
Protection of well-known marks (Article 6bis — basis of India's well-known trademarks), no
forfeiture for importation of patented articles, inventor's right to be named (moral right),
and temporary protection at international exhibitions.

7. KEYWORDS
Paris Convention, priority 12 months, national treatment, convention country Section 133,
independence of patents, Article 5A, well-known marks, exhibition priority.

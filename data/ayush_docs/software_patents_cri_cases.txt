SOFTWARE PATENTS IN INDIA — SECTION 3(k) AND LANDMARK CASES
Patents Act, Section 3(k) | CRI Examination Guidelines 2016/2017/2025

1. THE LAW
Section 3(k) bars "a mathematical or business method or a computer programme per se or
algorithms". The words "per se" (inserted on the Joint Parliamentary Committee's recommendation)
mean the bar covers programmes AS SUCH — not every invention implemented using a computer.
Business-method exclusion is absolute; software exclusion turns on substance.

2. THE CORE TEST: TECHNICAL EFFECT / TECHNICAL CONTRIBUTION
Examiners look at the SUBSTANCE of claims as a whole (method, system, device or readable-medium
form does not matter). A computer-related invention escapes 3(k) where it offers a technical
solution to a technical problem producing effects beyond the program itself — e.g., improved
memory management, network-level protocols, hardware control, signal processing.

3. FERID ALLANI v. UNION OF INDIA (DELHI HIGH COURT, 12 DEC 2019)
Application for a "method and device for accessing information sources and services on the web"
was rejected under 3(k) by the Patent Office and IPAB for want of technical effect. The High
Court reversed the approach: the bar applies to "computer programs per se", NOT to all
computer-based inventions; in an age of AI, blockchain and digital products it would be retrograde
to deny patents merely because a program is involved. Where technical effect/contribution exists,
the invention is patentable. The Court ordered re-examination under CRI Guidelines and precedents.

4. MICROSOFT TECHNOLOGY LICENSING v. ASSISTANT CONTROLLER (DELHI HIGH COURT, 15 MAY 2023)
For authentication technology using cookies and two-factor authentication, the Court held the
solution went beyond user-interface design into network-level communication — "closer to the
heart of computer and network technology" — hence patentable. Confirmed: 'per se' qualifies ONLY
computer programmes, while business-method exclusion is absolute.

5. GUIDELINES TIMELINE
2013 first CRI draft → 2015/2016 guidelines (2016 version strict: effectively required novel
hardware via a 3-step test) → 2017 revised guidelines (removed the hardware requirement; no
absolute bar on software claims; substance-over-form) → CRI Guidelines 2025 (current examination
framework with technical-effect analysis and illustrative examples).

6. DRAFTING TIPS
Claim the technical system/method with structural features and reference numerals; disclose the
technical problem, solution and effect in the specification; avoid pure algorithm/flowchart
claims; support both method and system claim sets.

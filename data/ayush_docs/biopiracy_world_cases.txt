WORLD-FAMOUS BIOPIRACY CASES — TRADITIONAL KNOWLEDGE UNDER THREAT
International IP and Traditional Knowledge Protection

1. WHAT IS BIOPIRACY?
Biopiracy means the appropriation of biological resources and associated traditional knowledge
without the prior informed consent of, and fair benefit-sharing with, the provider communities.
It is the central problem addressed by the CBD, the Nagoya Protocol, India's Biodiversity Act
(2002) and the TKDL defensive-protection system.

2. HOODIA (SOUTHERN AFRICA)
The San peoples of the Kalahari used Hoodia gordonii, a succulent plant, to suppress hunger and
thirst during long hunts. South Africa's CSIR isolated the appetite-suppressant compound (P57)
and licensed it to Phytopharm/Unilever without San consent. After exposure, a landmark 2003
benefit-sharing memorandum was signed with the San Council — the first major access and
benefit-sharing agreement for traditional knowledge in Africa.

3. TEFF (ETHIOPIA)
Teff (Eragrostis tef) is Ethiopia's staple grain used for injera. A Dutch company obtained European
patents relating to teff processing and flour, triggering accusations of misappropriation of
Ethiopian genetic resources. Ethiopia contested the patents and access terms under CBD principles.

4. AYAHUASCA (AMAZON)
In 1986 a US plant patent (PP5,751) was granted on a variety of Banisteriopsis caapi, the core
ingredient of the sacred Amazonian ayahuasca brew. Indigenous federations (COICA) challenged it as
lacking novelty since the plant and its uses were long known. The USPTO revoked the patent on
re-examination in 1999; after further proceedings it was reinstated in a narrowed form in 2001 —
leaving the case a lasting symbol of TK vulnerability in patent systems.

5. ENOLA BEAN (MEXICO)
A US patent (5,894,079) was granted to Pod-Ners LLC on a yellow bean ("Enola") essentially
identical to traditional Mexican mayocoba beans. Mexico's CIAT and farming groups challenged it;
after re-examination the patent's claims were rejected, protecting Mexican bean exporters.

6. BT BRINJAL — INDIA'S BIODIVERSITY ACT IN ACTION (2011)
India's National Biodiversity Authority initiated proceedings against Mahyco and others alleging
that local brinjal (eggplant) varieties were accessed for developing Bt brinjal without prior
approval under the Biodiversity Act, 2002. The case became the flagship domestic enforcement of
access and benefit-sharing obligations for agri-biotech research.

7. LESSONS FOR INDIA
(a) Document first: TKDL-style documentation converts oral heritage into searchable prior art.
(b) Approve access: Biodiversity Act approvals + NBA/SBB/BMC chain before research/commercial use.
(c) Share benefits: Nagoya-style mutually agreed terms and community funds.
(d) Watch foreign filings: monitor USPTO/EPO/WIPO publications for TK-based claims.

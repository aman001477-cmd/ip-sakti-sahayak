BONN GUIDELINES ON ACCESS AND BENEFIT-SHARING (2002)
CBD COP-6 Decision VI/24 | Precursor to the Nagoya Protocol

1. WHAT THEY ARE
Voluntary guidelines adopted under the Convention on Biological Diversity to help countries,
researchers and companies implement Articles 8(j), 15 (access to genetic resources) and 16
(technology transfer) — the bridge between CBD principles and the binding Nagoya Protocol (2010).

2. CORE ELEMENTS
(a) PRIOR INFORMED CONSENT (PIC) of the providing country and of indigenous/local communities
    holding associated traditional knowledge.
(b) MUTUALLY AGREED TERMS (MAT) covering benefit-sharing: monetary (royalties, fees, milestone
    payments) and non-monetary (technology transfer, training, joint research, capacity building).
(c) COMPETENT NATIONAL AUTHORITIES and checkpoints to monitor utilisation.
(d) INCENTIVES for users who comply; model contractual clauses for access agreements.

3. RELEVANCE TO INDIA
India's Biodiversity Act (2002) and the NBA approval system operationalise Bonn-style PIC+MAT
domestically; the Jeevani/Arogyapacha arrangement is a textbook Bonn-type success. The 2023
Amendment Act's simplified compliance tracks continue this lineage.

4. FROM BONN TO NAGOYA
Nagoya (2010) hardened Bonn's voluntary approach into binding obligations: access permits,
internationally recognised certificates of compliance, checkpoints, and user-country compliance
measures — all covered in nagoya_protocol.pdf in this knowledge base.

5. KEYWORDS
Bonn Guidelines, PIC, MAT, COP-6, voluntary, checkpoints, model clauses, capacity building,
Nagoya precursor.

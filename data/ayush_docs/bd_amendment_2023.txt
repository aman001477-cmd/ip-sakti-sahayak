BIOLOGICAL DIVERSITY (AMENDMENT) ACT, 2023 — WHAT CHANGED
Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, Government of India

1. CONTEXT
Parliament passed the Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act in August 2023 to simplify compliance,
promote ease of doing business, attract investment in biodiversity research, and decriminalise
minor defaults — while retaining benefit-sharing with communities and conserving resources.

2. DECRIMINALISATION OF OFFENCES
The original 2002 Act prescribed imprisonment up to five years for violations. The 2023 amendment
replaces criminal punishment with graded MONETARY PENALTIES (from about Rs. 1 lakh up to
Rs. 50 lakh depending on damage caused), adjudicated by designated officers (Joint Secretary
level), with appeals before the National Green Tribunal (NGT). Serious deliberate violations can
still draw higher penalties; the shift is from jail to fines for procedural defaults.

3. RELIEF FOR AYUSH, FARMERS AND COMMUNITIES
(a) Registered AYUSH practitioners, local communities and users of codified traditional knowledge
    are substantially exempted from the rigorous approval and benefit-sharing machinery for
    access-based activities.
(b) Cultivated medicinal plants are treated more leniently than wild/endangered biological
    resources, supporting the Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani and Homoeopathy supply chains.
(c) Collaborative research and biodiversity studies through government-approved institutions get
    simplified, fast-track pathways.

4. ACCESS, APPROVALS AND BENEFIT SHARING (SIMPLIFIED)
(a) Foreign entities and foreign-controlled companies still need NBA approval for access and for
    IPR based on Indian biological resources or associated knowledge.
(b) Domestic entities follow a lighter registration/intimation route in many cases, with benefit
    sharing determined through regulations rather than case-by-case negotiation.
(c) Benefit-sharing continues to flow to local communities, BMCs and the National/State
    Biodiversity Funds, including through community biodiversity registers.

5. WHAT STAYS STRICT
Prior informed consent principles, NBA oversight of foreign access, IPR disclosure obligations,
and protection of threatened species and biodiversity heritage sites remain intact.

6. PRACTICAL TAKEAWAYS FOR AYUSH STARTUPS AND RESEARCHERS
- Check whether your material is cultivated vs wild-sourced before assuming exemption.
- Document source, approvals and benefit-sharing terms from day one.
- For patents based on Indian biological resources, NBA clearance remains part of the checklist
  alongside Patent Office requirements.
- Track the updated NBA/SBB regulations and forms operationalising the amendment.

7. KEYWORDS FOR RETRIEVAL

plants, NBA approval, benefit sharing, ease of doing business, Biodiversity Act.

FAMOUS INDIAN GEOGRAPHICAL INDICATIONS & REGISTRATION GUIDE
Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999

1. WHAT IS A GEOGRAPHICAL INDICATION?
Under Section 2(e) of the GI Act, 1999, a geographical indication identifies agricultural, natural or
manufactured goods as originating in a territory where a given quality, reputation or other
characteristic is essentially attributable to its geographical origin. For manufactured goods, at
least one of the activities of production, processing or preparation must take place in the territory.

2. FAMOUS REGISTERED INDIAN GIs
(a) DARJEELING TEA — India's FIRST registered GI (Application No. 1, registered 2004). Grown in the
    Darjeeling hills of West Bengal. The Tea Board of India actively enforces it worldwide.
(b) BASMATI RICE — Registered as a GI covering the Indo-Gangetic plains across seven north Indian
    states. Protects India's premium long-grain aromatic rice in export markets after the historic
    RiceTec patent controversy in the USA.
(c) ALPHONSO MANGO (HAPUS) — Registered for the Ratnagiri-Sindhudurg belt of Maharashtra.
(d) KANCHIPURAM SILK SAREE — Registered for the traditional handloom silk of Tamil Nadu.
(e) PASHMINA — Registered for the fine hand-spun shawls of Kashmir.
(f) NAGPUR ORANGE, MALABAR PEPPER, JOHA RICE OF ASSAM, KOLHAPURI CHAPPAL, BANARASI SAREE,
    MYSORE SILK, CHANDERI FABRIC — examples of agricultural, handicraft and foodstuff GIs.
(g) AYURVEDIC AND HERBAL LINK: Several classical formulations and region-specific medicinal
    preparations derive reputation from traditional knowledge of a region; GI protection complements
    TKDL-based defensive protection by giving positive commercial rights to producer communities.

3. REGISTRATION PROCESS (GI ACT, 1999)
(a) WHO MAY APPLY (Section 11): Any association of persons, producers, organisation or authority
    representing the interest of producers of the concerned goods.
(b) APPLICATION: Filed with the Registrar of Geographical Indications (Chennai) specifying the
    goods, territory, quality-reputation link, and inspection structure.
(c) EXAMINATION, ADVERTISEMENT AND OPPOSITION: Similar in spirit to trademark procedure —
    examination, advertisement in the GI Journal, opportunity for opposition, then registration.
(d) AUTHORISED USERS: Individual producers/dealers within the territory must register as
    "authorised users" to use the GI tag alongside the registered proprietor.
(e) TERM: Registration is valid for TEN YEARS and renewable indefinitely (Section 18).

4. INFRINGEMENT AND ENFORCEMENT
Falsifying or falsely applying a GI, selling goods with false GI labelling, and misleading
indications of origin are punishable offences with imprisonment and fines. Registered proprietors
and authorised users can sue for infringement and pass off imitation goods (e.g., fake Pashmina,
non-Darjeeling tea sold as Darjeeling).

5. GI vs TRADEMARK vs PATENT (QUICK DISTINCTION)
- GI protects place-linked reputation collectively; no single owner; potentially perpetual.
- Trademark protects a trader's distinctive brand; owner-specific; renewable every 10 years.
- Patent protects a new invention exclusively for 20 years; then it enters the public domain.

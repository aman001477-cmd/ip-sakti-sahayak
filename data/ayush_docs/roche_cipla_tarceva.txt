ROCHE v. CIPLA (TARCEVA/ERLOCIP) — PUBLIC INTEREST IN PHARMA INJUNCTIONS
Delhi High Court 2012 | Patent Infringement and Interim Relief

1. BACKGROUND
Roche held an Indian patent covering Erlotinib, marketed as Tarceva for lung and pancreatic
cancer, priced at well over Rs. 1 lakh per month of treatment. Cipla launched the generic
Erlocip at a small fraction of that price. Roche sued for infringement and sought an interim
injunction to pull Cipla's product off the market during trial.

2. THE 2012 INTERIM ORDER — INJUNCTION REFUSED
The Delhi High Court declined the interim injunction, applying the classic three-factor test
with a pharma-specific plus-factor:
(a) PRIMA FACIE CASE / CREDIBLE VALIDITY CHALLENGE: Cipla raised a serious, arguable challenge
    to the patent's validity (covering obviousness/prior-art grounds), so the balance on
    validity was genuinely triable — courts do not grant injunctions lightly where validity is
    credibly in doubt.
(b) BALANCE OF CONVENIENCE: weighed with the price gap and patient access in view.
(c) PUBLIC INTEREST AS A THIRD PILLAR: in life-saving drug disputes, the court expressly
    factored the public interest in affordable access — an approach later echoed across Indian
    pharma jurisprudence. Irreparable injury to the patentee was not established to outweigh
    these considerations.

3. SIGNIFICANCE
(a) Established that in pharmaceutical injunction suits, PUBLIC INTEREST is an independent
    consideration alongside prima facie case and balance of convenience.
(b) Strengthened the litigation toolkit of generic companies: credible validity challenges +
    access-to-medicine arguments.
(c) Companion context to Bayer-Natco (compulsory licensing) and Novartis (Section 3(d)) in
    India's access-to-medicines jurisprudence — three different routes, one policy direction.

4. PRACTICE NOTE
The dispute also featured a revocation counter-claim and extensive claim-construction
debate on polymorph/salt coverage — draft pharma specifications and oppositions accordingly.

5. KEYWORDS
Roche Cipla, Tarceva erlotinib, Erlocip, interim injunction refused, public interest third
factor, credible validity challenge, balance of convenience, access to medicines.

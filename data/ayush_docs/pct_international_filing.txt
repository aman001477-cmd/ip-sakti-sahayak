PCT INTERNATIONAL PATENT FILING — GUIDE FOR INDIAN APPLICANTS
Patent Cooperation Treaty (WIPO), International Phase and National Phase

1. WHAT IS THE PCT?
The Patent Cooperation Treaty, administered by WIPO, lets an applicant seek patent protection in
150+ contracting states through ONE international application instead of many separate national
filings. India has been a PCT member since 1998. The PCT does NOT itself grant an "international
patent" — grant decisions stay with each national/regional office.

2. WHY INDIAN APPLICANTS USE IT
(a) DEFERS COSTS: National-phase fees and translations are postponed up to 30/31 months from the
    priority date, giving time to assess commercial value.
(b) ONE SEARCH: A single International Search Report (ISR) with written opinion previews
    patentability before expensive national filings.
(c) STRATEGIC: Useful for pharma, biotech, AYUSH-derived formulations and engineering inventions
    targeting US, EP, JP, CN and other markets alongside India.

3. THE INTERNATIONAL PHASE (MONTHS 0–30)
(a) FILING: File with the Receiving Office — Indian applicants may file at RO/IN (Indian Patent
    Office acting as receiving Office) or directly at RO/IB (WIPO), claiming priority from the
    earlier Indian application within 12 months.
(b) INTERNATIONAL SEARCH (Chapter I): An International Searching Authority (ISA) issues the ISR
    and written opinion (~16 months from priority).
(c) PUBLICATION: WIPO publishes the application at 18 months from priority.
(d) OPTIONAL CHAPTER II: File a Demand for international preliminary examination to get an
    International Preliminary Report on Patentability and extra time to amend claims.

4. NATIONAL PHASE (BY ~31 MONTHS)
Enter each desired country before its deadline — in India, 31 months from priority — by paying
national fees, furnishing translations, and appointing local agents. Indian national-phase
applications then undergo normal examination (request, FER, hearing, grant) under the Patents
Act, 1970, including Sections 3(d)/3(p) scrutiny for pharma and TK-linked inventions.

5. COST STRUCTURE (INDICATIVE)
International filing fee + search fee + handling fee (payable in CHF/USD equivalents), then
per-country national fees, translations and attorney charges. ePCT electronic filing reduces fees
and paperwork.

6. INTERPLAY WITH INDIAN LAW
Remember Section 39 (foreign filing permission for resident inventors), Section 8 (duty to
inform the Controller of corresponding foreign/PCT applications via Form 3), and TKDL/Biodiversity
Act clearance where biological resources or traditional knowledge are involved.

7. KEYWORDS FOR RETRIEVAL
PCT, WIPO, international application, priority 12 months, receiving office, ISA, ISR, Chapter II
demand, national phase 31 months, ePCT, Budapest Treaty microorganisms, Section 39, Form 3.
